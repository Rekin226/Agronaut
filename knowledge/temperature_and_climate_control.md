# Water Temperature and Climate Control

Water temperature sets the pace of the whole system: it drives fish appetite, bacterial activity, dissolved-oxygen
capacity, and plant growth. **Stability matters more than hitting a perfect number** — fish handle a steady 24 °C far
better than a tank that swings 24→30→24 every day.

## Match the fish to the climate (not the other way round)
- **Warm-water fish** (tilapia ~27–30 °C optimum) — cheapest to run in warm/tropical climates; struggle and stop
  feeding below ~15 °C.
- **Cold-water fish** (trout ~12–18 °C) — for cool climates or heated indoor spaces; die in warm water (low DO + stress).
- Picking a species suited to your ambient climate is far cheaper than heating or chilling water year-round.

## Why temperature couples to oxygen
- **Warm water holds less oxygen.** A hot afternoon plus a heavy feed plus night-time respiration is the classic
  low-DO, dawn-gasping trap — see `dissolved_oxygen_and_aeration`. In warm systems, aerate harder.

## Heating and cooling (in rough order of value)
- **Insulate and shade first** — insulate tank walls/lids, shade in summer, use the thermal mass of a large water volume.
  A big DWC sump buffers temperature swings for free.
- **Greenhouse** — extends the season and stabilises air temperature; watch for summer overheating (vents, shade cloth).
- **Water heaters / chillers** — effective but energy-hungry; size to the tank and use only when insulation isn't enough.
- **Solar / passive** — dark tanks and greenhouse gain in cool climates; shade and evaporative cooling in hot ones.

## Seasonal operation
- **As it cools:** fish eat less → **cut feed** (overfeeding a cold tank just fouls it), plant growth slows, nitrifying
  bacteria slow (biofilter capacity drops). Ease the stocking/feeding load in winter.
- **As it warms:** appetite and growth rise, but so does oxygen demand and disease pressure — raise aeration and watch DO.

## Watch the daily (diel) swing
- Large day–night temperature swings stress fish and can trigger disease. If your tank swings more than a few degrees
  daily, add water volume, insulation, or shade rather than chasing the thermostat.
