# Tilapia and Clarias in Hot, Dry Climates (Sahel, West Africa)

Tilapia and clarias (African catfish) are the two easiest fish for aquaponics in hot places like Burkina Faso.
Both like warm water, both are sold in local markets, and fingerlings are usually available.

## Which one should I choose?
| | Tilapia | Clarias (African catfish) |
|---|---|---|
| Best water temperature | about 27 to 30 °C | warm, similar range |
| Stops eating below | about 15 °C | tolerates cool nights better than it tolerates cold weeks |
| If the air pump stops | at risk within hours | breathes air at the surface, much safer |
| Temperament | calm, easy to handle | strong, can jump; needs a lid or net |
| Market | widely eaten | often sells well, smoked or fresh |

- **Unreliable power?** Clarias is the safer choice (FAO 589 names catfish as easiest where electricity is
  not reliable).
- **Want a calm, simple first fish and have backup air?** Tilapia.
- Do not mix them in one tank: clarias will eat small tilapia.

## Stocking
- A safe beginner level is **up to about 20 kg of fish per 1,000 litres** of tank water. Stay lower if your power
  is unreliable.
- Tanks under 500 litres: half that, and FAO 589 does not recommend growing table fish in them at all.
- Tilapia are usually stocked at about 50 g and harvested at about 500 g.
- Clarias tolerates much higher densities in professional systems, but only with strong solids removal. A
  beginner should not try that.

## Heat is the real enemy in the hot season
- Water above about 32 °C stresses the fish and holds little oxygen.
- **Shade the fish tank** (shade net, a roof, or burying part of the tank in the ground).
- Keep tanks covered against direct sun; this also stops algae.
- Feed in the cooler hours (early morning, late afternoon) and feed less on the hottest days.
- Increase aeration in the hot months.

## Cool nights (harmattan season)
- Water can drop at night. Tilapia eat less and grow slower below about 20 °C.
- Cover tanks at night, and reduce feeding when the fish show less appetite. Uneaten feed rots and fouls the water.

## Feeding
- Use a proper pelleted fish feed (around 30 to 35% protein is common for grow-out).
- Feed what they eat in a few minutes, two or three times a day, and remove leftovers after 30 minutes.
- Write down how much you feed. It tells you how well the fish are growing and how much your plants can take.

## Buying fingerlings
- Buy from a hatchery you trust, and look for active, evenly sized fish with no wounds or white spots.
- Keep new fish in a separate tub for about two weeks before adding them to the main system, to avoid bringing in
  disease.
