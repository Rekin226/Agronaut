# The First Weeks of a New System: When Can I Add Fish?

A new system is full of clean water but has no bacteria yet. The bacteria are what turn fish waste
into plant food, and they take weeks to grow. Adding a full load of fish on day one is the most common
way beginners lose their first fish.

## The short answer
- Plan on **about 4 to 6 weeks** before the system is ready for a full stock of fish. FAO 589 gives
  **25 to 40 days** in good conditions, and **up to two months** when the water is cool.
- The system is ready when **nitrite reads 0**, **ammonia is below 1 mg/L**, and **nitrate is rising**.
- You need a simple test kit (ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, pH). Without one you are guessing.

## What happens, week by week
1. **Days 1 to 10: ammonia rises.** You add a small ammonia source every day (a pinch of fish feed, or
   ammonia-based fertiliser without additives) to keep ammonia around 1 to 2 mg/L. Nothing seems to happen.
2. **Days 10 to 20: nitrite appears.** The first bacteria have arrived. Ammonia starts to fall and nitrite climbs.
3. **Days 20 to 40: nitrite falls, nitrate climbs.** The second group of bacteria has arrived. When nitrite
   is back to 0 and nitrate keeps rising, the cycle is done.

## A simple daily routine while you wait
- Run the pump and air all day, every day. The bacteria need oxygen.
- Add the small ammonia dose.
- Test ammonia and nitrite every 2 or 3 days and write the numbers down.
- Keep the water between about 25 and 30 °C if you can; cold water slows everything.
- You can plant hardy seedlings once nitrate appears. They will look pale at first; that is normal.

## Adding the fish
- Start with **a few fish**, not the full number, and add the rest over the following weeks.
- Acclimatise: float the fish bag in the tank for 15 to 20 minutes so the temperature matches, then let tank
  water into the bag slowly before releasing them.
- Expect a **small second spike** of ammonia or nitrite after stocking. If either goes above 1 mg/L, change
  part of the water (a third is a common amount) and feed less for a few days.
- Feed lightly at first: what the fish eat in a few minutes, and remove anything left after 30 minutes.

## Can I cycle with fish in the tank?
Yes, but it is harder on the fish and usually slower. Use only a few hardy fish (tilapia or clarias), feed
very little, test often, and change water whenever ammonia or nitrite passes 1 mg/L.

## Speed it up
Borrow a handful of gravel or filter material from a healthy, running system and put it in your biofilter or
media bed. It carries live bacteria and can cut weeks off the wait.

## Common first-month mistakes
- Adding all the fish at once.
- Using chlorinated tap water without letting it stand or treating it first (chlorine kills the bacteria).
- Overfeeding "to help the plants".
- Switching the pump off at night to save power.
