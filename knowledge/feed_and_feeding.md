# Feed and Feeding — the Engine of the System

In aquaponics, **fish feed is the only nutrient input for the whole system.** Everything the plants eat starts as
feed the fish process into waste. So feeding is not just fish husbandry — it is how you drive plant growth, and it is
the master lever behind system sizing (the **feeding-rate ratio**, g feed per m² of plant per day).

## The feed–plant link (why the rate matters)
- **Too little feed → starved plants** (nutrient deficiencies even in a healthy, cycled system).
- **Too much feed → pollution** (ammonia spikes, low DO, solids build-up). **Overfeeding is the single most common
  root cause of system crashes** — see `common_system_failures`.
- Match the daily feed to the plant area you're growing. Agronaut's model uses this ratio to size a balanced system.

## Protein and feed type
- **Grow-out tilapia / warm-water omnivores** — around **32% protein** pellets.
- **Fingerlings / young fish** — higher protein (~**40%**), smaller pellet size.
- **Carnivores (trout)** — higher protein still (~**40–45%**).
- Prefer **floating pellets** so you can watch intake and remove what isn't eaten. Match pellet size to mouth size.

## How much and how often
- Feed roughly **1–2% of body weight per day** for grow-out fish — but treat that as a guide, not a rule.
- Better method: **feed to satiation** — give what the fish clear in a few minutes, 2–3 small meals a day rather than
  one big one, and **remove any uneaten feed** after ~20 minutes.
- **Temperature drives appetite.** Fish eat far less when cold; feeding a cold tank at "normal" rates just fouls the
  water. Cut back as temperature drops. (Agronaut's model already applies a temperature feed factor when sizing.)
- Skip a day without worry if fish are stressed, after a water-quality event, or right after stocking.

## Feed quality and alternatives
- A quality commercial pellet gives a **predictable, balanced nutrient stream** for the plants — the easiest way to keep
  the system in balance.
- Supplements — **black soldier fly larvae, duckweed, azolla, worms** — can partially replace pellets and cut cost, but
  they shift the nutrient balance and are lower/variable in protein; introduce them gradually and watch plant response.

## Quick feeding checklist
- Are fish eating eagerly? (appetite is your #1 health signal)
- Any uneaten feed after 20 min? → you're overfeeding.
- Plants pale/deficient with clean water? → you may be under-feeding relative to plant area.
- Water gone cloudy / ammonia up after a feed change? → back off and let the biofilter catch up.
