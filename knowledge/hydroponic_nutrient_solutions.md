# Hydroponic Nutrient Solutions: Mixing, EC and pH (No Fish)

_Domain: hydroponics_

In hydroponics there are no fish, so the plants get all their food from fertiliser salts dissolved in the water.
You control the recipe directly, which makes growth fast and predictable, and mistakes show up quickly.

## Two numbers to watch every few days
- **EC (electrical conductivity)** tells you how much fertiliser is in the water, in total. It does not tell you
  which nutrients, so a correct EC can still hide a missing element (Goddek et al. 2019, ch. 4).
- **pH** decides whether the plants can take those nutrients up. Keep it around **5.5 to 6.5**. Iron is taken up
  best around 5.5 to 6.0; above about 7, iron and other micronutrients lock out and leaves yellow even when the
  nutrient is present.
- As a starting point, leafy greens such as lettuce are commonly grown at a lower EC than fruiting crops such as
  tomato. Follow the rate on your fertiliser label and adjust by watching the plants.

## Why fertilisers come in two parts (A and B)
Calcium nitrate and the sulphate or phosphate salts react when they are mixed **concentrated**, and form a solid
that settles out. The plants then get neither. So:
- Keep two stock solutions: **A** (calcium nitrate, often with iron) and **B** (the other salts).
- Add A to the tank water, stir, then add B. **Never mix A and B concentrates together.**
- Choose salts to fix one nutrient without overshooting another. For example, if only potassium is short, use
  potassium sulphate rather than potassium nitrate, which would also raise nitrate (Goddek et al. 2019, ch. 4).

## Keeping the solution right
- **Top up with plain water** as plants drink and water evaporates; top up with nutrient only when EC falls.
- If EC keeps **rising** while the water level drops, plants are drinking more than eating: top up with plain water.
- Replace the whole solution every few weeks, because unused elements build up and the balance drifts.
- Check your source water first. Hard water already contains calcium and magnesium; salty water (high EC before any
  fertiliser) is a problem for most crops.

## Adjusting pH
- To lower pH, growers use small amounts of acid; nitric or phosphoric acid are common and also add a nutrient.
- To raise pH, potassium hydroxide or potassium carbonate.
- Change it slowly, in small steps, and measure again after mixing.

## Warm climates
- Warm water holds less oxygen and roots rot. Keep the reservoir shaded and aerated, and keep the solution as
  cool as you can.
- In deep water culture, an air stone in every bed is standard.

## Hydroponics or aquaponics?
Hydroponics gives more control and needs no fish care, but you buy all the fertiliser and must dispose of old
solution carefully. Aquaponics feeds plants from fish waste and produces fish too, but is slower to start and
needs the bacteria to work. Agronaut can size either one.
