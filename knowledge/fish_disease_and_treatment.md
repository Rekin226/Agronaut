# Fish Disease and Treatment in Aquaponics

Most fish disease is a **water-quality problem first**. Before treating for a pathogen, rule out low DO,
ammonia/nitrite, pH swings, and temperature stress — a stressed fish gets sick, and fixing the water often
fixes the fish. Only after the environment is right should you treat for a specific disease.

## The aquaponics constraint (read this before dosing anything)
An aquaponic system is three living things sharing one water: fish, **nitrifying bacteria** (your biofilter),
and plants. Most standard fish medications will kill one of the other two:
- **Copper** — lethal to plants and to any invertebrates; never use it in a coupled system.
- **Antibiotics** — wipe out the biofilter bacteria; you can lose your whole nitrogen cycle overnight.
- **Formalin, malachite green, potassium permanganate** — harm the biofilter and are not food-safe on edible plants.
- Even many "organic" fish treatments stress the biofilter.

**Golden rule: treat sick fish in a SEPARATE quarantine / hospital tank**, not in the main system. There you can
use treatments freely without harming plants or bacteria, and you protect the rest of the stock.

## Aquaponics-tolerant tools (in the main system, cautiously)
- **Improve the environment** — raise DO, correct ammonia/pH, stabilise temperature. This alone resolves many cases.
- **Salt (non-iodised NaCl)** — a mild, effective anti-parasite/anti-stress aid, but **leafy greens are salt-sensitive**;
  prefer dosing salt in a hospital tank. Low doses only in a coupled system.
- **Temperature** — for ich, gradually raising temperature (within the species' safe range) speeds the parasite's
  life cycle so it can be cleared faster.

## Common diseases and what points to them
- **Ich / white spot** — tiny white "salt grains" on skin/fins; flashing (rubbing on surfaces). The most common disease.
- **Flukes / external parasites** — flashing, clamped fins, excess mucus, no visible spots.
- **Fungal (Saprolegnia)** — cotton-wool white/grey tufts, usually on already-injured or stressed fish.
- **Bacterial (fin rot, ulcers, red sores, columnaris)** — ragged fins, open sores, red patches; often follows poor water quality.

## Prevention (worth more than any cure)
- **Quarantine every new fish** for ~2–4 weeks in a separate tank before adding them.
- Keep DO high, ammonia/nitrite at zero, and avoid crowding and overfeeding.
- Don't share nets/equipment between systems without disinfecting.
- Observe daily at feeding: appetite and behaviour are your earliest disease signals.
