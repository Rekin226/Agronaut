# Power Cuts, Backup Air, and Running on Solar

In places where the grid goes off for hours, power is the biggest single risk to the fish. The pump moves the
water and the air pump puts oxygen in. When both stop, oxygen falls, and in a warm, well-stocked tank fish
can die **within an hour** (FAO 589).

## Priority order when the power goes off
1. **Air first.** Oxygen matters more than circulation for the first hours.
2. **Stop feeding** until power is back. Digestion uses oxygen and adds ammonia.
3. **Circulation second.** The bacteria in the biofilter also need oxygenated water; if the cut is long,
   expect ammonia to rise afterwards.

## If you have no backup right now
- Splash the water hard: pour it back in from a height, or spray it with a hose. Surface movement adds oxygen.
- Keep it going without long breaks.
- Move some fish into a bucket of clean water and splash that too, so fewer fish share the oxygen.
- Shade the tank. Warm water holds less oxygen.

## The cheapest insurance
- A **battery air pump** or an **AC/DC air pump** that switches to its battery when mains power fails. FAO 589
  recommends this combination for exactly this reason; simple battery backups have saved many systems.
- Keep it charged and test it every month by unplugging the mains.
- A small 12 V air pump on a car battery also works in an emergency, but only if it is set up before the cut.

## Choosing a design that survives cuts
- **Clarias (African catfish)** breathe air, so a sudden drop in oxygen does not kill them the way it kills
  tilapia. FAO 589 names catfish as the easiest fish where electricity is unreliable.
- **Lower stocking density** buys time: fewer kilograms of fish per litre means oxygen lasts longer.
- **Media beds** are more forgiving than NFT, where roots dry out within minutes when the pump stops.
- Avoid NFT if cuts are frequent.

## Running on solar
Pumps and air pumps can run entirely on solar power, with panels, an inverter and batteries for the night and
cloudy days. The panels are not the expensive part; the **batteries** are, because the air pump must run
24 hours a day.
- Size the battery for the **air pump through the night** first, then for the water pump.
- Many small systems run the water pump on a timer in daylight and the air pump all the time.
- Low-power DC pumps waste less energy than running AC pumps through an inverter.
- Ask a local installer for a quote; Agronaut's cost tool prices grid electricity, not solar kits.

## After the power comes back
- Check the fish, then test ammonia and nitrite the same day and the next.
- If ammonia rises above 1 mg/L, change part of the water and feed lightly for a few days.
