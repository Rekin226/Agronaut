# Starting Small on a Budget

You do not need an expensive kit to learn aquaponics. A small media-bed system built from reused containers
teaches you everything, and mistakes cost little. Start small, get one full growing season right, then grow.

## The simplest design: a media bed on top of a fish tank
- **Fish tank:** one food-grade container, ideally **500 litres or more** if you want to eat the fish. FAO 589
  does not recommend growing table fish in less than 500 litres. For a first trial with a few fish, smaller works.
- **Grow bed:** a shallow container above or beside the tank, filled with gravel.
- **One small pump** lifts water from the tank to the bed; it drains back by gravity.
- **One air pump** with an air stone in the fish tank.
- The gravel bed does three jobs at once: holds the plants, traps fish waste, and houses the bacteria. No
  separate filters are needed.

## Cheap containers that work
- **IBC totes** (the 1,000 litre cube tanks in a metal cage): cut one to make a tank plus a bed. FAO 589 includes
  build plans for IBC units.
- Old bathtubs, plastic barrels, or strong plastic tubs.
- **Only food-grade containers.** Ask what the container held before. Never use one that carried chemicals,
  oil or pesticides; rinsing does not make it safe.
- Anything outdoors must survive sun and heat: cover or paint plastic that sits in full sun.

## Growing media
- **Volcanic gravel 8 to 20 mm** is the classic choice: cheap where available, lots of surface for bacteria, and
  almost chemically inert. Smaller gravel clogs; bigger gravel gives less surface and poor plant support.
- Wash it well before use.
- Avoid limestone gravel (it keeps pushing the pH up). A simple test: drop vinegar on a stone; if it fizzes, do not use it.

## What to spend on, and what to save on
- **Spend on:** a reliable pump, a good air pump (with battery backup if power is unreliable), a basic water test kit.
- **Save on:** containers, stands (blocks and timber), plumbing made from standard PVC.

## A realistic first system
- One tank, one or two beds, a few kilograms of fish, and leafy greens (lettuce, amaranth, herbs) that grow fast
  and forgive mistakes.
- Keep a notebook: feed per day, test results, what you planted and when.

## When to grow
Once you have run the system through a full season with healthy fish and steady harvests, you know your water,
your climate and your market. That is the time to scale up, and to ask Agronaut to size a bigger system.
